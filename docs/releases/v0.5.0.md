Hands-free Chintu: a wake word, instructions that survive pauses, and a crash fix.

## Install

```sh
curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mohyt/chintu/main/install.sh | bash
```

On the phone, open this release and install `chintu-0.5.0.apk`; it installs over 0.4.x and keeps your pairings. The APK is now about 61 MB because it carries the offline wake-word model.

## New

- **Wake word "Chintu"** (Settings → Conversation → Wake word): an on-device listener (Vosk, offline) that works with the screen off or the phone locked. Say "Chintu", wait for the chime, then speak. It opens Chintu over the lock screen when "Display over other apps" is allowed, and its notification has a Turn off button. No audio leaves the phone until you speak after the chime.
- **Long instructions are no longer cut off** at the first pause (Android 13+): Chintu reads the microphone itself and sends the instruction after about two seconds of quiet. Measured: a 39-word instruction with two pauses arrived as one task instead of 13 words.

## Fixed

- The app crashed when you returned to it quickly while a task was running (`ForegroundServiceDidNotStartInTimeException`).
- After a wake from the lock screen, the instruction was lost while the app reconnected to the computer.

## Docs

- The README is now an end-user guide with screenshots for the phone and Ubuntu, pairing steps, permission profiles and troubleshooting.

## Known limits

- The phrase "chin two"/"chin to" can wake Chintu. No voice barge-in while it speaks.
- After an app update or a phone restart, open Chintu once to start the wake word again.
- Instructions are sent about two seconds after you stop speaking.

Device evidence: `docs/validation/2026-10-17-wake-word.md`.
